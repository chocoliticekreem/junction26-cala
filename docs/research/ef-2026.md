## 3. How the network works after the programme
- **Demo Day in SF** to 200+ VC partners, with companies already incorporated in the US and living in the Bay Area.
- **Follow-on capital from EF itself** (up to $5M through Series B), plus $70M of fund capital in the new raise.
- **Investor-backers of EF** (Collisons, Hoffman, Schmidt, Greylock, Songhurst) as a warm path.
- **Peer community and alumni** (600+ companies), 9 months of SF office space, partner credits.

## 4. Pain points and gaps (rewritten)

What is no longer true: "EF founders hit a wall after Demo Day." EF restructured around exactly that problem (SF relocation, US Demo Day, follow-on, tier-1 US investor access) and claims it halved time to raise. Do not pitch Cala as fixing EF's Demo Day.

What remains hard, for **EF founders** (inferred unless cited):
- Matching and cull risk is unchanged: roughly half of a cohort ends unpaired in participant accounts (see ef.md section 1).
- Access is cohort-shaped. The network is strongest for ~10 SF Demo Day companies per cycle and thinner for European and Indian founders until they relocate. Critics say relocation "reduces the supply of high quality companies... from Europe" ([Sifted via search](https://sifted.eu/articles/entrepreneurs-first-us-shift)).
- After seed, EF's coverage fades: Series A and beyond, hiring and customer intros need new graph work. The $5M follow-on is capped and applies to the portfolio selectively [inference].
- Investor choice beyond EF's own list: which of 200+ partners actually leads their sector and stage, and who the warm path to them is. No public tool for this from EF [UNCERTAIN, absence of evidence].

For **non-EF founders** (the larger group): no cohort, no Demo Day, no partner intros, no stipend; they rely on cold email and personal luck. EF's own acceptance rate is ~3% (third-party), so 97% of applicants do not get the network.

## 5. Pitch implications
- Position Cala as **network infrastructure for the founders EF does not take**, and as the research layer for EF's own investors and founders beyond Demo Day, not as an EF replacement.
- Strongest EF-adjacent use cases: sector and stage-specific lead-investor mapping with warm paths (post-seed, Series A), and edge-scouting lead lists for talent investors. Weakest: cofounder matching (EF owns the cohort mechanic).
- Safe claims: EF is a $1.3B company with a $24B claimed portfolio, led by a partner team, and has solved a lot of access for those inside it. Unsafe claims: "EF founders get stuck after Demo Day", "EF is a Clifford network".

**Accurate one-line contrast:** "EF turns about 3% of applicants into a founder network with a SF Demo Day and follow-on capital; everyone else, and every EF founder past the seed, still has to map investors and warm paths by hand."
