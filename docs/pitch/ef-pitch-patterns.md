# What makes an EF Demo Day pitch land

Corpus: 32 pitches. 15 from EF April '26 Demo Day, SF ([pbevtn0Beg4](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4)), 15 from Summer '24 Demo Day, SF ([r8coLpqM9eI](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8coLpqM9eI)), Fabric standalone ([z8kWAPDkSzY](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8kWAPDkSzY)) and inPact, an older pitch from 2020 used as the counter-example ([KT4i5C9SVfQ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KT4i5C9SVfQ)). Source text is YouTube auto-captions, so a few words are garbled. Timestamps are to the minute.

Shape: about 90 seconds and 250-300 words each. Average sentence length in the April '26 pitches is **10-13 words** (Nook 9.3, Layered 10.0, Elliot AI 13.2). The EF host does the warm-up. The founder walks on and starts with the punch.

## 1. First 10 seconds: a number, a moment or a wound. Never the company name.
Not one of the 30 recent pitches opens with "We are X, a platform for Y". Four openings recur:
- **A stark number:** "1 BILLION HOURS. That is the amount of video we need to make general-purpose robots work." (Hyphen Box, [16:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=960s)). "Seven trillion dollars of invoices sit idle and unfinanced in the US right now." (Iridium, [19:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=1140s)).
- **Traction as the first line:** "We just closed a one hundred thousand dollar pilot with the largest injury law firm in the world." (Specter, [25:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=1500s)). "One of our customers was going to hire 25 more marketers. Instead they hired our software." (AMT, [32:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8coLpqM9eI&t=1920s)).
- **A wound:** "When I lost my dad... I spent 27 months settling his estate." (Eversettled, [08:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=480s)).
- **A cheeky provocation:** "The average hotel sucks." (Nook, [14:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=840s)). "Big tech companies know you... better than our moms." (Fabric, [00:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z8kWAPDkSzY)). "You can build 10 unicorns by replacing the compliance function of just one big bank." (Elliot AI, [31:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=1860s)).

The counter-example shows the gap. The 2020 inPact pitch opens: "impact is an enterprise software platform designed to [unlock] business insights from contracts invoices and transaction documents..." It is accurate and nobody remembers it.

## 2. The problem has a name and a number
Pitches name a person: "Meet Michael. He's a data privacy officer who [vibe-]coded a risk management platform... his application broke with just 20 users, making him lose a contract worth $50,000." (Numeraia, [34:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=2040s)). "This is the Beast. Our customer Joe uses this arcane spreadsheet..." (Toolkit, [75:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8coLpqM9eI&t=4500s)). "Meet [Cissé], our first customer" (Loro; caption garbles the name). The number comes straight after, and the bigger it is the plainer the wording: "168 billion dollars a year paying humans to click buttons" (Layered).

## 3. "Why us" is one lived credential plus one vivid co-founder detail
The credential always maps to the problem, and the best ones are odd:
- "Before this, I wrote death policies for the UK government." His co-founder "spent 2 years in the German special forces. He's even built software for morgues." (Eversettled, [09:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=540s))
- "I was rejected by 300 VCs before raising 5 million dollars to open the UK's first robotic restaurant." (Nook, [15:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=900s))
- "When I worked in a Formula 1 mission control... we coordinated 20 mechanics to perform a pit stop in 2 seconds." (Kyber, [38:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=2280s))
- "My co-founder Prisha is nuts." (Iridium, [21:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=1260s)). "Michael's been building ERPs since he was 12 years old." (Toolkit)

inPact spends 50 seconds on a full CV (Linklaters, BCG, Oracle, PhD, three universities). The SF pitches spend about 10 seconds on credentials.

## 4. Traction is time-boxed, stacked and delivered in fragments
- "In just 11 weeks, we have 163 doctors under contract. $300,000 in contractual ARR. Just this week, we added $100,000." (Layered, [12:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=720s))
- "Every single pilot converted. Not in months, in weeks." (Lavender, [42:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=2520s))
- "That's contracted. That's binding." (Nook)
- "We went from $2,000 of volume in July to $4 million in September annualized." (Loro, [45:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8coLpqM9eI&t=2700s))
- "Our first angel investors are our customers." (Lavender)

Pre-revenue teams swap revenue for proof of momentum: "we filed our first patent this week" (Genie), "our sensor works in human clinical ranges" (Level Zero), "here is our first prototype" (Iris Lab, holding it up).

## 5. Rhythm: short lines, triplets, fragments
Three-beat lists and verbless fragments do most of the work: "27 months of phone calls, paperwork, courts, and banks." "Types claims, sits on hold, fights denials, gets dentists paid." (Lavender). Sentences rarely run past 15 words. Each number gets its own sentence.

## 6. The close: an inevitability line, not an ask
None of the 30 founders states a round size. The only use of "raising" in the corpus is Nook's 300-VC story. The ask is "Come find us" (Hyphen Box) or "Come talk to us" (Kyber). The final line is a category claim, often built on an analogy:
- "Epic became the largest software company in healthcare by owning the medical record. Layered will become the next one by owning the work." ([13:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pbevtn0Beg4&t=780s))
- "12 and a half thousand rooms would make us a unicorn. We're not stopping until we're bigger than Marriott's 1.5 million rooms." (Nook)
- "What Scale was to computer vision, Hyphen Box is to robotics."
- Wordplay closers: "let's get this ever settled" (Eversettled), "we're here to tame the Beast" (Toolkit), "the future of color isn't synthetic, it's fungal" (Petri).

## 7. Humour and personality
The humour is self-deprecating and quick, and it never costs more than one line. Kio: "someone actually said to me, why can we see your toes... as an American not named Emily in Paris, I knew I needed some help" ([64:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r8coLpqM9eI&t=3840s)). Genie: "Genie might be putting them out of business", said about her former employer. Personality comes from specifics such as morgues, toes, kayaking and the Beast, not from adjectives.

## What they never do
- Open with the company name and a category definition.
- Say "excited", "we hope", "as you can see" or "revolutionary platform". "Excited" appears once in 32 transcripts, and an EF host says it.
- Explain the technology for more than one sentence. "Lasers as emitters and photocells as receivers" (Iris Lab) is the whole technical explanation.
- State the round size or valuation.
- Hedge a number. Every figure is stated flat: "$1.5 million of annualized run rate."
- List features. Each pitch names one product job.
