# Pitch draft (working name: Cala Galaxy — check the name with a Cala mentor before using it publicly)

Status: draft. Voice follows the EF Demo Day pattern study in `ef-pitch-patterns.md`: open on a raw number or a moment, one blunt line on what we do, then a plainly stated, outrageous ambition. No mechanics in the voiceover; the demo explains.

## Rules
- Opening: 15 seconds max, then straight into the Cala interview.
- Every number on screen comes from the real product run on verified data. Anything unverified is marked [VERIFY] and does not go on camera.
- Do not use the Slush 68% / 30% stat (it is about customers, not investors).
- Tractable: "backed by EF", not "led by EF", until the data agrees.

## Opening (pick one, 12-15 s)
1. **The number**
   "Fifty-eight per cent of European founders say their biggest problem is money. Not product. Not hiring. Money. We're putting an end to this. Every investor in Europe, every round they've led, running as a simulation. Issey, let's run Cala."
   (Source: Slush Startup Struggle Survey 2025, 58.1%. "Every investor in Europe" overclaims slightly; the demo shows the real scale.)
2. **The moment** (fill with a real story; never invent it)
   "Last year I pitched [N] investors. [N] said no. [Most] were never going to say yes, and I had no way of knowing. So we cloned them. Issey, you're first."
3. **The ambition** (recommended: its last line is the first interview question)
   "Every unicorn in Europe started as a guess. A founder, a pitch, a gamble on which investor says yes. We're not stopping until it stops being a guess. Issey, where's Cala in five years?"

## The Cala interview (about 60 s on screen)
Setup: company name "Cala" already typed; Room has matched it in Cala's graph ("founded 2025 · Barcelona · backed by …" from verified data only).
1. "Tell us what Cala does, like you would to an investor." (mic on; transcript and chips stream live; aim for ~15 s)
2. "Where is Cala in five years?" (aim for "a unicorn"; the vision line appears: "Unicorn = $1B+ valuation")
3. Simulate. Let the screen carry it: possible realities best to worst, the forks (next lead A vs B, now vs later), the precedents behind each, next moves. One optional follow-up to Issey: "Which of these would you bet on?" — his reaction is the moment.
Prompt sheet for the shoot: keep questions verbatim, no explanation from us while the futures render.

## Investor side (about 40 s)
- One line: "Now the investors see it too."
- Pick a real fund (one with dense data in the cohort, e.g. Lightspeed or Seedcamp [VERIFY coverage]); its taste appears from its own deals; the rising stars light up.
- Open one company: "You're a plausible next lead in [X]% of its futures" [VERIFY: real output], precedents chips.
- Flash the "what moved and why" dashboard (only if built and real).

## Close (5 s)
- "Cala mapped every round that happened. We play out the ones that haven't."
- Alternates: "Your next round has already happened. Thousands of times." / "Every round in Europe is a star. We show you the path through them."
